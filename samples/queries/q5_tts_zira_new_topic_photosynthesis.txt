In this lesson we will look at photosynthesis, the process plants use to make their own food. Inside the leaves there are tiny structures called chloroplasts that contain a green pigment, chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs red and blue light from the sun. The plant takes in carbon dioxide from the air through small pores called stomata, and water from the soil through its roots. Using the energy of light, it splits water molecules, releases oxygen as a by-product, and builds glucose from carbon dioxide in a cycle of reactions known as the Calvin cycle. The glucose is used for energy and to build cellulose for new cell walls. That is why almost all life on Earth, including us, ultimately depends on photosynthesis.
