Today I want to explain how sourdough bread works. Instead of commercial yeast, a sourdough starter is a living culture of wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria that you feed with flour and water every day. When you mix the starter into the dough, the yeasts produce carbon dioxide which makes the loaf rise, while the bacteria produce lactic and acetic acids that give the bread its sour taste. A long, cold fermentation in the fridge develops more flavour and makes the gluten easier to digest. Bakers judge the dough by its volume, its bubbles and how it springs back when pressed. Finally the loaf is scored with a blade and baked in a very hot covered pot, so steam keeps the crust soft at first and lets the bread expand before it turns golden and crunchy.
